

## Generalization is Overrated


Why is generalization big thing financially? From a technical standpoint, it is groundbreaking. Why from a financial sense?

Let's look at scaling, specifically profit. Profit is simply I get more out than I put in. It is fundamentally unfair, and exploits inefficiencies where possible. Let us take a look at two curves

1. the investment curve (time vs money)
2. the revenue curve (time vs money)

Assume the revenue is already adjusted for the cost of revenue

If say along a 5 year period, I take the integral of both curves and take the difference, then 
1. (revenue - investment) > 0 => profit
2. (revenue - investment) = 0 => break even
3. (revenue - investment) < 0 => loss

Okay, revenue is 

`R_general(t) = R_legal(t) + R_medical(t) + R_coding(t) + R_support(t) + ... + R_n(t)`

Same area under the investment curve, multiplied area under the revenue curve. That's economies of scope in one line: one asset, many revenue streams.
