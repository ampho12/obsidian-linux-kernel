**Two incumbents and why their model breaks:**
- **JR Automation (Hitachi subsidiary)** — bespoke custom cells, 6–12 month build cycle. Amortizes over 5–10 year product cycles in auto/aerospace/medical. Doesn't fit a 12-month Nvidia cycle.
- **Cohu / Delta Design** — socket-specific component insertion. Built for Intel LGA / AMD SP5 sockets that ship 3–5 years × millions of units per SKU. Nvidia ships 12 months × ~100K units per SKU. Amortization math doesn't close.

**Structural reason:** fixed automation amortizes bespoke engineering across a product lifecycle. Nvidia's lifecycle is too short for the math to work.

The generalist bet is a foundation-model effort — pretrain on everything before deploying on anything. Long-term R&D, not near-term product.

- **Karol Hausman, Physical Intelligence ($1B+ raised):** *"only if you resist every commercial pressure pushing you toward specialization."*

Generalization solves everything before solving anything. Doesn't deliver in time for VR200 ramp.

## Technical Approach

### The problem

Move a 300lb server 2m to the rack, and insert it with sub-mm accuracy.

### Three technologies, three precision regimes

**Tier 1: Pretrained VLA (2m → 2cm)** — gross manipulation, navigation, approach.
*Proof:* π₀, GR00T, OpenVLA all work at this regime. Not yet production-grade.

**Tier 2: Specialized vision model (2cm → 1mm)** — precision visual servoing.
*Proof:* Models with 1000x less size and 1000x less data achieve 1mm performance.

**Tier 3: Force-torque control (micron level)** — safety-critical insertion and seating.
*Proof:* Industry-standard for decades (FANUC, Universal Robots + Robotiq). We data-tune the force profile.

### Hard challenges — non-trivial engineering

1. **Handoff orchestration.** Confidence thresholds, recovery paths, knowing when each layer is reliable. System engineering, not ML.
2. **Precision vision model.** Multi-camera fusion, calibration, disciplined data collection. Foundation-model teams aren't building this because it isn't "general."
3. **Force-aware learned policy.** F/T sensors are mature; force-aware learned policies are research-frontier. We are productizing this.

### Benefits

**1. Precision in bits, not atoms.** Fixed automation stores spatial knowledge in physical artifacts — fixtures, calibrated paths, machined tooling — that iterate on fab-shop lead times. We store it in model weights that iterate on training time.

**2. Adapts to the moving target.**

| Rack change | Fixed automation (atoms) | Our approach (bits) |
|---|---|---|
| New rail geometry (MGX 1.1 → 1.2) | Re-machine fixture, re-teach trajectory, recalibrate vision: **weeks–months** | Collect demos, fine-tune, validate: **days** |
| Board layout shuffle (same socket, new position) | Redesign nest, reprogram approach, retune force window: **weeks** | Fine-tune precision-vision head: **days** |
| New cooling block (GB300 → VR200) | New gripper, new force profile, new motion path: **weeks** | Fine-tune force-torque policy: **days** |

**3. Same primitive across tasks.** Move → align → insert is the action behind connector mating, module insertion, tray-into-rack, fastener seating, cold-plate mounting. Geometry changes per task; the primitive doesn't.

### Core technology

The vast majority of mechanical assembly is move-align-insert. That is our core technology.
